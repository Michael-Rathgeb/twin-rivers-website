Seamless gutters: 5-inch or 6-inch?

A 5-inch seamless gutter is right for most St. Louis houses; a 6-inch gutter is better when one run drains a large or steep roof area. The size comes from the roof, not the house: how many square feet drain into that run, how steep the roof is (rain hitting a steep roof lands on more surface), and how hard it rains locally. That's the method behind the sheet-metal industry's sizing manual from SMACNA.

The capacity gap is real. This Old House's K-style sizing guide rates a 5-inch gutter for about 5,520 sq ft of roof and a 6-inch gutter for about 7,960 sq ft, roughly 44% more. Simpler tables give lower numbers, but they agree on the direction.

We usually suggest 6-inch gutters when:

  • a valley dumps two roof planes into one short stretch of gutter
  • the roof is steep, so water comes off fast and overshoots a small gutter
  • a long run can only have one downspout
  • the old 5-inch gutter overflows in heavy rain even when it's clean

The rule of thumb for gutters and downspouts

The rule of thumb for gutters is no more than 40 feet of gutter per downspout, with the gutter pitched slightly toward each one; that's the maximum run Angi's gutter sizing guide gives. Downspouts are usually the bottleneck, not the gutter: a 3-by-4-inch downspout has a 12-square-inch opening, twice the 6 square inches of a 2-by-3, so upsizing the downspouts often fixes an overflowing gutter.

How much does gutter installation cost?

Gutter installation cost is set by the linear foot, plus the downspouts. The total depends on how many feet of eave the house has, how many downspouts it needs, whether old gutters come off first, and how many stories the crew works at. We measure, then give you the gutter installation cost in writing.

How much does it cost to replace gutters on a house?

Start with the footage, because that's what you pay for. A 1,500 sq ft single-story house built as a 30-by-50-foot box has about 100 feet of eave if it has a gable roof (gutters on the two long sides) and about 160 feet if it has a hip roof (gutters all the way around). Same house size, 60% more gutter. Add the downspouts, tear-off of the old system and any fascia repair, and that's the job.

How much do 200 feet of gutter cost?

200 feet of gutter costs 200 times the per-foot rate, before downspouts. On a house that size, expect at least five downspouts at the 40-foot rule, and each one is priced separately. A 60-foot run is the same math at a smaller scale, but it needs at least two downspouts.

Should you replace 20-year-old gutters?

Replace 20-year-old gutters if they leak at the seams, sag, pull away from the fascia, or overflow because they were undersized from the start; keep them if they're tight, pitched and draining. Age alone isn't the test. Replace them sooner if:

  • they're sectional gutters with leaking joints every 10 feet
  • the fascia behind them is soft or rotted
  • they were dented by hail; our page on hail damage repair covers adding them to a claim
  • you're replacing your roof, since new drip edge and gutters go on together

Gutter repair and downspouts

Gutter repair fixes the run you have when the gutter itself is sound. We handle:

  • leaking seams, end caps and corner miters
  • sagging sections and loose hangers
  • downspouts that are crushed, disconnected or too small
  • downspout extensions and underground drains that carry water away from the foundation
  • rotted fascia boards behind the gutter

Water that overshoots or backs up behind a gutter shows up elsewhere. Stained walls that end in siding replacement, and fascia rot we find while repairing roof leaks, often start at a clogged gutter.

Gutter guards

Gutter guards cut down on clogs; they don't end gutter maintenance. Fine debris, seed pods and shingle grit still collect, so plan on checking guarded gutters. Ask about guards at your free assessment and we'll tell you whether your trees and roof make them worth it.

Gutter FAQs

What is the little known trick for gutters?

Add downspouts before you upsize the gutter. A gutter that overflows is often draining toward too few outlets, and one more downspout, or a 3-by-4 in place of a 2-by-3, costs far less than a new run.

What is the average cost of gutters for a home?

It depends mostly on footage. Count the eaves that need gutters, multiply by the per-foot rate, then add downspouts and tear-off. A free assessment gives you the real number for your house.

Do seamless gutters really leak less?

Yes, along the runs. Seamless gutters are formed in one piece per run, so the only joints are at corners and end caps. Sectional gutters have a seam every section, and every seam is a place to leak.

Should gutters go on before or after a new roof?

After, or at the same time. New drip edge goes on with the roof, and the gutter hangs behind it. If you're getting a new roof soon, do both together.
