Emergency roof tarping: what happens on the first visit

Twin Rivers Contracting LLC provides emergency roof tarping. St. Louis storms usually leave a damaged area we can cover in one visit:

  1. We check the attic and ceilings to see where water is getting in.
  2. We photograph the damage on the roof before anything covers it.
  3. We clear loose debris from the damaged area where it's safe to.
  4. We fasten a heavy tarp over the opening and well past it, so wind can't get under it.
  5. We write up what the permanent repair will need.

Emergency roof tarping holds until the permanent repair, not for good. Sun and wind wear a tarp down, so we schedule the real fix as soon as the claim or estimate allows.

How urgent is roof repair after a storm?

It's urgent if water is getting in, and your policy expects you to act. The standard homeowners policy form (ISO HO 00 03, the model many insurers follow) lists "protect the property from further damage" among your duties after a loss, including "reasonable and necessary repairs to protect the property" and "an accurate record of repair expenses." A tarp is that repair. Waiting can let an insurer argue that later water damage wasn't covered.

  • Call now: water coming in, an open hole, or a tree or limb on the roof.
  • Call this week: missing or lifted shingles with no leak yet.
  • Book an inspection: granules in the gutters or dents in vents after hail.

For the last two, a free roof inspection tells you what's there.

Who pays for an emergency roof tarp?

Usually your homeowners insurance, as part of the claim, when the damage came from a covered event such as wind, hail or a fallen tree. Keep the photos and the tarp invoice and send both with your claim. If the damage came from age or a long-term leak, the tarp is on you, just as the repair would be. Our guide to filing a hail claim in Missouri covers the deductible and how the adjuster prices the roof.

Will insurance cover a 20-year-old roof?

Insurance may still pay for storm damage on a 20-year-old roof, but many policies pay older roofs at actual cash value, which subtracts depreciation, instead of full replacement cost. Some policies use a roof schedule that pays less as the roof ages. Check the roof section of your declarations page before you file.

What to do before the crew arrives

  1. Stay off the roof, especially when it's wet or dark.
  2. If water is near light fixtures or outlets, switch that circuit off at the breaker.
  3. Put buckets under drips and move furniture, rugs and electronics out of the way.
  4. If a ceiling is bulging, put a bucket under it and poke a small hole at the low point so it drains in one place.
  5. Photograph the ceilings, walls and anything wet, with the time on the photos. Once everything dries out, our handyman work covers drywall and trim repairs.
  6. Report the damage to your insurance company and write down the claim number.

From tarp to permanent repair

Once the tarp is on, the permanent fix is either a roof repair in the damaged area or a new roof, depending on what the inspection and the claim show. Small damage goes to roof repairs in St. Louis; damage across the roof goes to a full replacement.

Keep the emergency work separate on paper. Under Section 407.725 of the Revised Statutes of Missouri, you can cancel a contract for insurance-paid work until midnight of the fifth business day after your insurer tells you in writing that all or part of the claim isn't covered. If you cancel, the contractor may keep the reasonable value of emergency work you acknowledged in writing as necessary. Signing off on the tarp as emergency work, apart from any repair or replacement contract, keeps that line clear for both sides.

How to choose an emergency roof repair contractor

  1. They photograph before they tarp. Your adjuster needs to see the damage, not the tarp.
  2. The emergency work has its own invoice, so you aren't signed into a full roof job at 10 p.m.
  3. They're local and insured, and will still be around for the permanent repair.

Emergency roof repair FAQs

How much does a new roof cost in Missouri?

It depends on roof size in squares, pitch, material, tear-off layers and decking, so the honest number comes from measuring the roof. Our page on what a new roof costs in St. Louis shows how to work out your roof's size and compare estimates.

What are the different types of roofs?

On St. Louis homes, mostly asphalt shingles (three-tab and architectural) and some metal. Flat and low-slope roofs, common on City row houses and small commercial buildings, use membranes such as TPO, EPDM or modified bitumen. Each is tarped and repaired differently.

How do I get my roof replaced for free?

The only legitimate way is a covered insurance claim, where the insurer pays for storm damage minus your deductible. A contractor who promises a "free roof" by absorbing your deductible is a red flag: Missouri law (RSMo 407.725) bars contractors from offering to pay or rebate any part of your insurance deductible.
