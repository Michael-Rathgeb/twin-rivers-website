Roof replacement in St. Louis

A roof replacement is the right call when damage is spread across the roof, leaks keep coming back after repairs, or the shingles are worn out. Roof replacement in St. Louis also has a code side. Local building codes here are based on the International Residential Code, which doesn't allow new shingles over a roof that already has two layers or over one that's water-soaked or deteriorated. That's one reason our roof replacement is always a tear-off. If a fix in one spot would do, our St. Louis roofing team will say so and point you to roof repairs in St. Louis instead.

Should a 20-year-old roof be replaced?

Not because of the number alone. Replace a 20-year-old roof when it shows the wear:

  • Shingles curling, cracking or lifting
  • Bare spots where the granules have washed off
  • Soft spots in the decking underfoot
  • Leaks that come back after each repair

One money reason to look sooner: many policies pay older roofs at actual cash value, which subtracts depreciation, so a storm claim may pay well short of a new roof.

What is the 25% rule for roofing?

The 25% rule for roofing says that if more than 25% of a roof is repaired or replaced within 12 months, the entire roof must be replaced and brought up to current code. The 25% rule for roofing comes from the Florida Building Code. Is the 25% rule for roofing a Missouri rule? No. St. Louis roofing codes follow the International Residential Code, which has no 25% rule. The roofing rule that applies here is the layer limit in IRC Section R908.3.1.1, which bans a re-cover over two existing layers or over a water-soaked or deteriorated roof.

  • Florida: the 25% rule for roofing applies under the Florida Building Code.
  • Missouri: there is no statewide 25% rule for roofing.
  • St. Louis City and County: IRC-based codes with a layer limit, not a percentage.

The 25% rule for roofing is why a partial repair in Florida can turn into a whole new roof. In St. Louis, the choice between roof repair and roof replacement comes down to condition, layers and matching shingles, not a 25% rule.

How much does a new roof cost in St. Louis?

A new roof in St. Louis costs what its size, pitch, material and tear-off add up to, so the real number comes from measuring your roof. A new roof with architectural shingles costs more than one with basic three-tab shingles, and a new metal roof costs more than either. The roof replacement cost St. Louis homeowners pay depends on roof size in squares, roof pitch, material, tear-off layers and decking repairs. What is the average cost of a new roof in Missouri?

A statewide average blends ranch homes with two-story homes and three-tab shingles with metal, so it says little about your roof. Two numbers decide your new roof cost: how many squares the roof has (one square is 100 square feet of roof surface) and the installed price per square.

Turn the house size into roof size first. A one-story roof covers about the floor area; a two-story roof covers about half of it. Then multiply the flat footprint by the pitch factor:

  • 6/12 pitch: × 1.12
  • 8/12 pitch: × 1.20
  • 12/12 pitch: × 1.41

Is $30,000 too much for a roof?

It depends on what the $30,000 buys. Divide the price by the number of squares to get a price per square, then compare estimates on that number for the same scope: same material, same layers torn off, same underlayment and flashing. An estimate that won't say how many squares it priced is the problem, not the total.

What is the most expensive part of replacing a roof?

On most shingle roofs, the biggest line is the roofing material and the labor to install it, priced per square across the whole roof. The lines that surprise people are decking and extra layers, because nobody sees them until tear-off.

Does homeowners insurance pay for roof replacement in Missouri?

Homeowners insurance in Missouri pays for roof replacement when a covered event such as hail or wind damaged the roof badly enough to require replacement; it does not pay to replace a roof that wore out with age. You pay your deductible, and your policy decides whether the roof replacement is paid at replacement cost or actual cash value. How long does a roof replacement take?

A roof replacement takes a set number of working days that we write into your estimate before the roofing crew starts. Roof replacement time depends on roof size, roof pitch, weather and how much decking needs repair. Every roof replacement follows the same order:

  1. Protect the house, landscaping and driveway.
  2. Tear off the old roof down to the deck.
  3. Replace soft or rotted decking.
  4. Install underlayment, ice and water barrier, and flashing.
  5. Install the new shingles, ridge caps and vents.
  6. Clean up the yard and driveway, then walk the job with you.

Often yes, but it depends on where the house sits. The City of St. Louis runs its own building division. In St. Louis County the permit comes from your city or, in unincorporated areas and cities that contract with it, the County's Department of Transportation and Public Works.
