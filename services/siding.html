Siding replacement

Siding replacement St. Louis homes need is often smaller than people expect. This is a brick town: on many houses the siding is only on the dormers, gables, a rear addition, an enclosed porch and the soffit and fascia. So we scope siding replacement by wall, not by house. A gable end 24 feet wide with an 8-foot rise is a 96 sq ft triangle, about one square of siding (a square is 100 sq ft). A frame house sided on every wall is many times that.

Full replacement makes sense when panels are failing on several walls, the color can't be matched, or the sheathing behind is wet. A repair makes sense when the damage is on one wall and matching panels are still available.

Older homes and lead paint

If your home was built before 1978 and the old siding or trim is painted wood, the EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting Rule applies once the job disturbs more than 20 sq ft of exterior paint. The work has to be done by an EPA-certified firm using lead-safe practices: containment, no open-flame burning and a careful cleanup. Ask any siding contractor for their firm certification before work starts.

What is the best type of siding for a St. Louis house?

The best type of siding for a St. Louis house is the one that fits the wall, the budget and how much upkeep you want: vinyl for low cost and no painting, fiber cement for durability and a painted look, engineered wood for a wood look at a lower weight. Installed correctly, each one handles Missouri's hot summers and freeze-thaw winters.

What siding should you avoid?

Avoid any siding installed without the clearances its manual calls for, whatever the brand. In practice, the failures we replace are thin vinyl nailed tight, wood products touching the ground or a roof, and siding with no house wrap or flashing behind it.

What are the downsides of composite siding?

Engineered wood and other composites look like wood but don't forgive water. Cut ends must be sealed or painted, and the siding needs clearance above the ground, roofs and decks, or the edges swell. Fiber cement is heavy and has to be repainted over the years.

How much does it cost to replace siding?

Siding replacement is priced by the square (100 sq ft of wall) and by material, plus trim, soffit and fascia. So the cost to replace siding depends on how many walls are sided, not just the house's size. What moves the price:

  • material: vinyl costs less than fiber cement or engineered wood
  • tear-off, and any soft sheathing found underneath
  • trim work around windows, doors and corners
  • soffit and fascia replaced at the same time
  • height: second-story gables need more setup

Storm-damaged siding and insurance

Hail cracks and punctures vinyl, chips fiber cement and dents aluminum trim, usually on the walls that faced the storm. If the roof took hail, check the siding on the same side. We photograph the damage and write a scope for your adjuster; our page on filing a hail claim in Missouri explains deductibles and timing. If a storm also broke glass, our replacement windows can go in with the siding.

Common vinyl siding installation mistakes

Most vinyl siding failures are installation mistakes, and the Vinyl Siding Institute's installation guidance covers each of them:

  • Nailing too tight. Vinyl has to slide as it expands, so the nail head sits about a dime's thickness off the panel. Tight nails cause ripples.
  • Nails off-center. Each nail goes in the center of the slot, straight, not at an angle.
  • No expansion gap. Leave at least 1/4 inch at J-channel, corners and openings, and 3/8 inch when installing below 40°F.
  • Caulking the panels into corners or J-channel, which locks them in place.
  • Face-nailing through the panel instead of the nailing hem.
  • Skipping flashing at windows, doors and roof-to-wall joints.

Roof-to-wall flashing is shared with the roof, so siding and roof leak repair often get done together, along with seamless gutter installation when the fascia is replaced.

Siding FAQs

What is a common mistake to avoid during vinyl siding installation?

Nailing the panels tight. Vinyl moves with temperature, and a panel that can't slide ripples in the first hot spell.

Which siding brands are best?

The installation matters more than the label. Any major brand fails if it's nailed tight or flashed wrong. Ask which product lines a contractor installs, whether they follow that manufacturer's manual, and who registers the warranty.

Can new siding go over old siding?

Sometimes, over a flat, dry wall. We open a few spots first: if the sheathing is soft, the old siding comes off.

Do you replace soffit and fascia with the siding?

Yes. Soffit and fascia are usually done with the siding so the trim matches and the gutters hang on sound wood.
