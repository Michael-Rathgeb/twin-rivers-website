What St. Charles roofs face: storms, hail and freeze-thaw

  • Hail, on the record: NOAA's Storm Events Database lists 83 reports of hail 1 inch or larger in St. Charles County from January 2016 through April 2026, on 30 separate days. The largest was a 4-inch report near St. Charles on May 11, 2016.
  • Big single days: on May 16, 2025, the county logged 30 of those reports in one afternoon, up to 2.5 inches near Cottleville.
  • Wind: straight-line winds lift shingle tabs and ridge caps, and once the seal breaks the next storm finishes the job.
  • Freeze-thaw: water that gets under a lifted shingle freezes and widens the gap every winter.

Do you need a permit to replace a roof in St. Charles?

Usually not for shingles. The City of St. Charles Building Division lists replacing roof shingles among the projects that generally don't require a permit. The exception is the historic districts, including South Main, Historic Downtown and Frenchtown: exterior work there may need approval from the city's Historic Landmarks Preservation and Architectural Review Board first, and the district guidelines set what roofing fits. Frenchtown's, for example, allow asphalt, wood, slate or synthetic slate shingles but not roll roofing visible from the street. Roofing questions from St. Charles homeowners

How do I pick a roofer?

Ask for a certificate of liability insurance, a written and itemized estimate, photos of the damage, and who pulls the permit. Be wary of anyone who asks for full payment before work starts.

Is $30,000 too much for a roof?

It depends on the number of squares, the pitch, the material and the decking. Compare written estimates line by line, on the same material and the same number of squares, before you decide a price is high.

Will insurance cover a 20-year-old roof?

It can, if a covered storm damaged it. Many policies pay older roofs at actual cash value, which subtracts depreciation, instead of full replacement cost. Your declarations page says which applies.

What is the 25% rule for roofing?

It's a Florida Building Code rule about repairing more than a quarter of a roof. Missouri codes have no matching percentage, so the roof's condition and age decide repair versus replacement here.
