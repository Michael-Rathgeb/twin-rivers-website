Replacement window types and materials

  • Double-hung: both sashes slide, and most tilt in for cleaning. The usual choice for older St. Louis homes.
  • Casement: hinged on the side and cranked open; seals tightly when closed.
  • Sliding: opens sideways, good for wide, short openings.
  • Picture: fixed glass for light and views.
  • Bay and bow: project out from the wall to add space and light.
  • Awning: hinged at the top, so it can stay open in light rain.

Do you need a permit to replace windows in St. Louis?

It depends on where you live, because permits are set by each city and county in the St. Louis area, and the City of St. Louis is separate from St. Louis County. Two rules are worth knowing before you order:

  • City historic districts. In the City of St. Louis's local historic districts, exterior changes such as replacement windows are reviewed by the City's Cultural Resources Office, which publishes a list of replacement windows it has approved for those districts.
  • Bedroom escape windows. Codes based on the International Residential Code exempt a replacement window from the escape-opening size rules if it goes into the existing opening and is the largest standard size that fits, in the same operating style or one with an equal or larger opening.

Is it better to install replacement windows from the inside or outside?

Either can be right; it depends on the house. An insert replacement keeps the existing frame and is often set from the inside after the interior stops come off, which leaves brick and exterior trim untouched. A full-frame replacement takes the window out to the rough opening and is done when the frame is rotted or you want the full glass size back. Insert is faster and less disruptive; full-frame lets us fix rot and flash the opening properly.

Lead-safe rules for older homes

In homes built before 1978, the EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting Rule treats window replacement as covered work no matter how small, because old sashes and trim often carry lead paint. The job must be done by an EPA-certified firm with containment and a lead-safe cleanup. Ask any window contractor for their firm certification.

What is the cheapest time of year to replace windows?

Late fall and winter are often the slowest months for exterior work, so scheduling is easier then, and windows can be replaced in cold weather one opening at a time so the house is only open briefly. Don't wait for a tax credit: the federal energy-efficient home improvement credit, which covered up to $600 a year for windows, ended for windows installed after December 31, 2025, under the federal tax law signed in July 2025 (the One Big Beautiful Bill Act).

Which replacement window brands are best?

The best replacement window is the one rated for your climate and installed well. Brand matters less than three things you can check yourself:

  1. The NFRC label: a lower U-factor means less heat loss; solar heat gain coefficient shows how much sun heat gets through.
  2. ENERGY STAR certification for your climate zone, which ENERGY STAR's zone finder shows by county.
  3. The installation: a top-rated window leaks if it isn't flashed and sealed.

Window replacement FAQs

What is the best company for new windows?

One that measures every opening, explains how the window will be installed and sealed, and puts the price and warranty in writing. Compare quotes window by window.

How long does window replacement take?

Insert windows go in one at a time, so each opening is only open briefly. The total time depends on how many windows you're replacing and whether any frames need repair.

Should I replace all my windows at once?

Not necessarily. Many homeowners start with the worst windows or one side of the house. Doing them together keeps the look consistent.

Can you replace a window without replacing the frame?

Yes, if the frame is sound. That's an insert replacement. If the frame or sill is rotted, a full-frame replacement is the better fix.
