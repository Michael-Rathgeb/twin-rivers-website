What does a roof inspection include?

A roof inspection includes the roof surface, everything that seals it, everything that drains it, and the attic underneath. Ours covers:

  • Shingles and roofing material: cracks, curling, missing pieces, granule loss and hail bruising.
  • Flashing: seals at chimneys, vents, skylights and walls, where most leaks start.
  • Gutters and drainage: whether water leaves the roof and the house.
  • Attic ventilation: intake and exhaust vents, and signs of trapped heat or moisture.
  • Structure: sagging, uneven decking and soft spots.
  • Interior signs: water stains, daylight through the boards, wet insulation.

How attic ventilation is checked

Ventilation has a number behind it. The International Residential Code (Section R806.2) calls for 1 square foot of net free vent area for every 150 square feet of attic floor, or 1 for every 300 when the vents are split between the eaves and the ridge as the code describes. A 1,500 sq ft attic needs 10 square feet of vent area at 1/150, or 5 at 1/300. An attic well short of that runs hot in summer and damp in winter, which ages shingles and decking from underneath.

What fails a roof inspection?

A roof fails an inspection when it can't keep water out or won't for much longer. The common reasons:

  • Active leaks or wet decking
  • Soft, sagging or rotted decking
  • Missing, cracked or badly curled shingles
  • Flashing that has pulled loose at a wall or chimney
  • Hail bruises that have cracked the shingle mat
  • Heavy granule loss, with bare spots on the shingles

Five signs your roof needs to be replaced

  1. Leaks keep coming back in different spots after repairs.
  2. Shingles are curling, cracking or losing granules across whole slopes, not one area.
  3. The decking feels soft underfoot or sags between rafters.
  4. You can see daylight through the roof boards from the attic.
  5. The roof already has two layers, so the next fix can't be another layer.

One of these on one slope usually means roof repairs in St. Louis, not a new roof. Several across the roof is when replacing your roof costs less than patching it every year.

Roof inspections after hail and wind

After a storm, a roof inspection does two jobs: it finds the damage, and it builds the record your insurer will ask for. We photograph each slope, mark hail hits and wind-lifted shingles, and check the soft metal (vents, gutters, flashing) where hail leaves dents you can measure. If water is already coming in, emergency roof tarping comes first.

An inspection before storm season helps too. Dated photos of a sound roof make it easier to show that new damage came from a specific storm, not from age. Our page on hail damage repair walks through the claim.

Pre-sale roof inspections and roof certifications

Before a home sale, a pre-sale roof inspection tells the seller what a buyer's inspector will find and tells the buyer what the roof will need. Three things in the report matter most to a sale:

  • How many layers it has. Under Section R908.3.1.1 of the International Residential Code, a roof with two layers can't take a third, so the next roof is a full tear-off.
  • Active leaks and any soft decking.
  • Storm damage that could still be claimed under the current owner's policy.

If a buyer or lender asks for a roof certification, tell us when you book so we know what the document needs to say.

Roof inspection FAQs

What is the 25% rule for roofing?

It's a Florida Building Code rule: repair or replace more than 25% of a roof within 12 months and the whole roof must meet current code. St. Louis-area codes follow the International Residential Code, which has no such percentage. Our roof replacement page explains what does apply here.

Will a roof inspection find a leak?

Usually, when the attic is checked as well as the roof. Water runs along decking and rafters before it drips, so the stain on your ceiling is rarely under the hole. Matching the attic to the roof surface finds it.
