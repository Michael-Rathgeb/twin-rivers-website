How to measure your roofline for lights

  1. Measure the eaves, the level edges where the gutters hang, with a tape or a measuring wheel along the ground below.
  2. Add the gables, the sloped edges. A sloped edge is longer than the width under it: on a common 6-in-12 roof pitch it's about 12% longer, so a 30-foot-wide gable has two edges of about 16.8 feet, roughly 33.5 feet in total.
  3. Keep the stories separate. First-story and second-story footage are priced differently.
  4. List the trees and bushes you want lit.

Clips, not nails: protecting your roof and gutters

Christmas lights should be clipped to the shingles and gutters, never nailed or stapled. A nail or staple through an asphalt shingle leaves a hole that water can follow into the roof deck, and pulling stapled wire down in January can tear shingle edges and gutters. Shingle maker CertainTeed's holiday-decorating guidance recommends plastic clips that grip the shingle edge or gutter lip instead. Ask any installer how the lights will be attached.

Lights that hook on the gutter add weight to it, so loose hangers and leaking seams are worth fixing first; gutter cleaning and gutter repairs before the lights go up keep leaves from packing behind the clips. If you're not sure the roof is in shape, a free roof inspection on the same visit tells you, and loose or sagging runs may need new seamless gutters.
