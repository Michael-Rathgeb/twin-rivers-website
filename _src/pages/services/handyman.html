What a handyman can and can't do in St. Louis

Missouri has no statewide handyman license, so the line is drawn by permits. In St. Louis County, plumbing permits are issued to master plumbers and drainlayers licensed by the County, and electrical permits to County-licensed electrical contractors. Cities that run their own permit offices, such as University City, ask plumbers for that County license too.

  • Handyman work: repairs and replacements at the fixture, with no new pipe, drain or wiring run.
  • Licensed-trade work: new water or drain lines, water heaters, gas lines and new electrical circuits. Those need a licensed plumber or electrician and usually a permit.

If something on your list crosses that line, we'll say so before we start, so you can call the right trade for that one item.

What is a solar panel critter guard?

A critter guard is a wire mesh fitted around the edge of rooftop solar panels to close the gap between the panels and the roof. Without it, birds and squirrels nest in that sheltered gap, where nesting debris holds water against the roof and animals can chew the panel wiring. The mesh clips to the panels, so it adds no holes in the roof.

Handyman FAQs

Can a handyman do plumbing in Missouri?

Minor plumbing at the fixture, yes: a faucet, a toilet, a supply line to a fixture. New lines, drains, water heaters and gas work need a licensed plumber; in St. Louis County, plumbing permits go only to County-licensed master plumbers and drainlayers.

Is it cheaper to hire a handyman or a contractor?

For a list of small jobs, a handyman is usually cheaper, because one person handles every item in one trip. For one large job, like a new roof or a wall of siding, hire the contractor who does that job every day.
