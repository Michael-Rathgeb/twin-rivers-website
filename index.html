Roofing for St. Louis City and St. Louis County

We are roofers St. Louis City and St. Louis County homeowners both call, and the difference between the two matters. The City of St. Louis is an independent city, separate from St. Louis County, and the two run separate building departments. That matters for roofing because permit rules, fees and inspections depend on exactly where the house sits. A home in the City answers to the City's Building Division. A home in St. Louis County answers either to its own municipality or, in unincorporated areas and in the cities that contract with the county, to the county's Department of Transportation and Public Works.

Roofing in St. Louis City means brick homes, many with flat or low-slope rear roofs. Roofing in St. Louis County means mostly asphalt shingle roofs on subdivision homes. Our roofers work on both, and on the commercial flat-roof work found on storefronts and small offices across both.

Do you need a permit for a roof in St. Louis County?

Often yes for a full roof replacement, and usually not for a small repair, but the answer depends on your municipality. St. Louis County has dozens of separate cities, and a 2021 St. Louis REALTORS® white paper counted no fewer than 42 different building codebooks in use across the county. Before a replacement, the contractor should confirm the rule with your city or the county and pull any permit that's required.

Do you have to be licensed to fix a roof in Missouri?

No. Missouri does not issue a statewide roofing contractor license, so roofing licenses and roofing permits are handled locally by individual cities and counties in the St. Louis area. Because Missouri has no statewide roofing license, ask any roofer for proof of liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage and check the permit rules where you live.

How do I choose a roofing contractor in St. Louis?

Choose a roofing contractor in St. Louis who is local, licensed and insured, and who gives you a written estimate before work starts. Ask for proof of insurance, a written scope of work, the warranty terms in writing, and local references. Avoid out-of-town storm chasers who show up after hail storms. Roofing companies St. Louis homeowners can rely on will also explain the inspection photos and answer questions about decking, flashing and ventilation, not just shingle color.

Red flags: roofing companies St. Louis homeowners should avoid

  • They won't put the price, the scope or the warranty in writing.
  • They want a large deposit before materials are even ordered.
  • They offer to "cover" or waive your insurance deductible. Missouri law (RSMo 407.725) bars contractors from offering to pay or rebate any part of your insurance deductible.
  • They show you damage photos but won't take you up on the roof or show where the photos were taken.
  • They have no local address or phone, and no reviews older than the last storm.
  • They push you to sign the same day, before your insurance adjuster has looked at the roof.

We'll show you every photo from your inspection, explain what each one means, and give you the price in writing; that's how Twin Rivers works on every roof.

St. Louis roofing FAQs

How much does a roof inspection cost in St. Louis?

A roof inspection from Twin Rivers Contracting LLC costs nothing: every roof inspection in St. Louis is free, with no obligation. The free roof inspection covers shingles, flashing, gutters, attic ventilation and signs of leaks, and you keep the photo report and written recommendations whether or not you hire us.

Who are the best roofing companies in St. Louis?

The best roofing companies St. Louis homeowners hire are local, insured, put the price and warranty in writing, and can show finished roofs near you. Check each roofing company's Google reviews, ask for a certificate of insurance, and get written estimates from two or three roofing companies for the same scope so you compare like with like.

Will insurance cover a 20-year-old roof?

Insurance may still pay for storm damage on a 20-year-old roof, but many policies pay older roofs at actual cash value, which subtracts depreciation, rather than full replacement cost. Some policies have a separate roof schedule based on age. Read the roof section of your declarations page, or ask your agent, before you file.

Does a 30-year roof really last 30 years?

Not usually. The "30-year" label describes the shingle's warranty class, not a promise of 30 years on your house. Real life depends on attic ventilation, installation quality, sun exposure and storms. In a hail-prone area like St. Louis, a roof is often replaced after a storm well before its rated life is up.

How do people get a roof replaced for free?

The only legitimate way is a covered insurance claim, where the insurer pays for storm damage minus your deductible. A contractor who promises a "free roof" by absorbing your deductible is a red flag: Missouri law (RSMo 407.725) bars contractors from offering to pay or rebate any part of your insurance deductible. You still owe the deductible under your policy.
